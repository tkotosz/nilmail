nilmail privacy policy

Last updated 25 November 2026

nilmail is a small app for sorting a Gmail inbox with swipes. It is not open to the public: only Google accounts its owner has invited can sign in, each with their own account. This page explains what nilmail does with your data.

Who can sign in

  • Only invited Google accounts. The owner keeps the list of invited email addresses in nilmail's server settings. That list is the only thing the owner sees about you: they cannot see your mail, your labels or your settings.
  • Signing in takes two steps. First Google asks you only to share your email address. If that address is not on the list, nilmail withdraws that access at once, keeps nothing, and you are not asked for Gmail. Only an invited account goes on to the second step, where Google asks for access to Gmail.
  • Google warns that nilmail is not verified. That is because it is a small private app for fewer than 100 people the owner knows, which Google treats as personal use; apps for the public go through Google's verification instead.
  • When the owner takes your address off the list, your access ends within an hour: the server stops renewing it and withdraws nilmail's access to your Google account.

What nilmail can access

When you sign in with Google and agree, nilmail gets:

  • your Google account's email address, to check that you are invited;
  • access to your Gmail (Google's gmail.modify permission), to show the conversations of the piles you set up and to archive them, mark them read, add or remove labels, or move them to the Bin when you swipe. When you name a new label in Settings, nilmail creates it in your Gmail. It cannot delete mail permanently. Google describes this permission as reading, writing and sending email; it is the narrowest one that allows archiving, but nilmail never writes or sends email.

Where your mail goes

  • Your browser fetches your mail directly from Google and shows it on your device. Your mail is never sent to nilmail's server or stored anywhere else.
  • Your settings, such as your piles, what each swipe does and which labels it uses, are saved in your browser only.
  • nilmail has no analytics, no trackers and no ads.

What the server handles

nilmail runs on Cloudflare. Its server part only keeps you signed in. After you sign in, your browser holds a cookie with your email address and your Google sign-in, encrypted so that only nilmail's server can read it. About once an hour, your browser sends that cookie to the server. The server checks that your address is still invited, gets a fresh one-hour access key from Google and passes the key back to your browser. Nothing is written to a database or stored on the server.

  • Cookies: the sign-in cookie lasts up to 400 days, or until you sign out. While you sign in, a second cookie that lasts 10 minutes makes sure the answer from Google belongs to your sign-in. nilmail sets no other cookies.
  • Like any web host, Cloudflare handles the requests to nilmail's address, such as your device's internet address. See Cloudflare's privacy policy. Mail never passes through it.

Images in emails

When an email contains pictures from the internet, nilmail loads them while you read the email, unless you turn that off in Settings. As in any mail app, the sender can then see that the email was opened.

Unsubscribing

Many newsletters say in their email how to unsubscribe. When you tap Unsubscribe, nilmail uses that: your browser sends the sender's unsubscribe link a request straight from your device, opens the sender's unsubscribe page in a new tab, or opens a prepared unsubscribe email in your mail app for you to send. The sender learns that you unsubscribed and, like any website you visit, your device's internet address. Nothing goes through nilmail's server.

Sharing

nilmail does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone, and does not use it for advertising. Nobody reads your mail.

nilmail's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

Choose Sign out in nilmail's settings: this withdraws nilmail's access at Google and deletes the cookie. You can also remove nilmail any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Your browser's saved settings go away when you clear the site's data. To be taken off the list of invited addresses, ask the owner.

Questions

Ask the person who invited you to nilmail.
